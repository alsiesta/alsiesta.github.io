Physics Notes

Classical Mechanics

Newton's Laws of Motion

  1. First Law (Inertia): An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion unless acted upon by a force
  2. Second Law: Force equals mass times acceleration (F = ma)
  3. Third Law: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction

Key Formulas

Basic motion equations:

  • Velocity: v = d / t
  • Acceleration: a = Δv / t
  • Force: F = ma
  • Momentum: p = mv
  • Kinetic Energy: KE = ½mv²
  • Potential Energy: PE = mgh

Energy and Work

Work-Energy Theorem

Work done on an object equals the change in its kinetic energy:

W = ΔKE = KE_final - KE_initial

Conservation of Energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another.

Total Energy = KE + PE = constant (in isolated systems)

Waves and Sound

Wave Properties

  • Wavelength (λ): Distance between wave peaks
  • Frequency (f): Number of waves per second (Hz)
  • Amplitude: Maximum displacement from equilibrium
  • Speed: v = fλ

Sound

Sound is a mechanical wave that requires a medium to travel. Speed of sound in air is approximately 343 m/s at room temperature.

Electricity and Magnetism

Ohm's Law

The relationship between voltage, current, and resistance:

V = IR

  • V = Voltage (volts)
  • I = Current (amperes)
  • R = Resistance (ohms)

Power

Electrical power formulas:

  • P = VI
  • P = I²R
  • P = V²/R

Important Constants

  • Speed of light: c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s
  • Gravitational acceleration: g = 9.81 m/s²
  • Universal gravitational constant: G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N⋅m²/kg²
  • Planck's constant: h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J⋅s

Study Tips

  1. Understand concepts before memorizing formulas
  2. Draw diagrams to visualize problems
  3. Always include units in calculations
  4. Check if your answer makes physical sense
  5. Practice problems regularly
