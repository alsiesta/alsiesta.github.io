Chemistry Basics

The Periodic Table

The periodic table organizes all known elements based on their atomic number and chemical properties.

Element Groups

  • Alkali Metals: Group 1 (e.g., Li, Na, K)
  • Alkaline Earth Metals: Group 2 (e.g., Mg, Ca)
  • Halogens: Group 17 (e.g., F, Cl, Br)
  • Noble Gases: Group 18 (e.g., He, Ne, Ar)
  • Transition Metals: Groups 3-12 (e.g., Fe, Cu, Au)

Chemical Bonds

Types of Bonds

  • Ionic Bonds: Transfer of electrons between atoms (e.g., NaCl)
  • Covalent Bonds: Sharing of electrons between atoms (e.g., H₂O)
  • Metallic Bonds: Electrons shared among many atoms (metals)

Electronegativity

The tendency of an atom to attract electrons in a chemical bond. Fluorine is the most electronegative element.

Chemical Reactions

Types of Reactions

  1. Synthesis: A + B → AB
  2. Decomposition: AB → A + B
  3. Single Replacement: A + BC → AC + B
  4. Double Replacement: AB + CD → AD + CB
  5. Combustion: Fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O

Balancing Equations

The law of conservation of mass requires that the same number of atoms of each element appear on both sides of a chemical equation.

Example: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

States of Matter

Phase Changes

  • Melting: Solid → Liquid
  • Freezing: Liquid → Solid
  • Vaporization: Liquid → Gas
  • Condensation: Gas → Liquid
  • Sublimation: Solid → Gas
  • Deposition: Gas → Solid

Acids and Bases

pH Scale

Measures acidity/basicity on a scale from 0 to 14:

  • pH < 7: Acidic (e.g., lemon juice, vinegar)
  • pH = 7: Neutral (e.g., pure water)
  • pH > 7: Basic/Alkaline (e.g., soap, bleach)

Common Acids and Bases

Acids:

  • Hydrochloric acid (HCl)
  • Sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄)
  • Nitric acid (HNO₃)

Bases:

  • Sodium hydroxide (NaOH)
  • Calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)₂)
  • Ammonia (NH₃)

Mole Concept

A mole is 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number).

Key Formulas

  • Moles = Mass / Molar Mass
  • Molarity (M) = Moles / Liters
  • Density = Mass / Volume

Study Tips

  1. Memorize common elements and their symbols
  2. Practice balancing equations regularly
  3. Understand electron configurations
  4. Use the periodic table effectively
  5. Work through stoichiometry problems
